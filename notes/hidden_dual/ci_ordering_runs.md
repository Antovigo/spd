# CI-ordering runs: what exists, how each is specified, and why

Every run launched while testing `CI_hidden >= CI_output` — live, cancelled, and the
measurement jobs behind the design. Design reasoning lives in
[`monotone_ci_plan.md`](monotone_ci_plan.md); this file is the run ledger.

## The question

The two CI nets score the same subcomponent pool. A subcomponent reaches the model's output
only through the output of the matrix it lives in — which is exactly what the hidden
objective measures — so **output-important should imply hidden-important**, while the
converse may fail freely for a component whose contribution is cancelled downstream. A
violation is the `ab_grids` "magenta" signature: the output decomposition leaning on
something that does no work at its own site. The series asks whether enforcing the ordering
produces a more mechanistically faithful decomposition, and — the part that turned out to
matter most — **which net should absorb the correction**.

## Control

**`addsub-L18-11-trunk-imp2x`** — finished 20001 steps, applets built, from the earlier
impmin sweep. Config: `~/pd_scratch/impmin_sweep/addsub-L18-11-trunk-imp2x.yaml`.
Shared-trunk dual CI, `uniform_k_subset` routing, SmoothL0 impmin at `coeff 5e-5` with a 2x
peak held to 70% of training, γ annealing 1.0 → 0.01 from 50%.

Every live arm differs from it by **one field**, verified field-by-field with `model_dump()`
(`~/pd_scratch/monotone_ci/check_configs_v3.py`), so no separate baseline arm is needed.

One caveat on reusing it: the code now has the two CI roles share a binomial jitter draw,
where this run drew independently per role. That changes only the *correlation* between the
two nets' mask dither, not either net's marginal — and the two objectives run on separate
forward passes with independently drawn sources regardless.

### `floor` result (complete)

20001 steps, exit 0, peak GPU 41.1 GiB. Over all 41 evals the **shortfall read exactly 0.0**
— the constraint held from step 0 to 20000 with no drift and no assert, which is the
end-to-end check that the mechanism works. `recon` fell 0.097 → 0.00237;
`NAlive_hidden` fell monotonically to 1677.

One reading trap: `impmin_hidden` bottoms near 55.4 at the halfway mark and then *rises* to
64.3. That is the γ anneal (1.0 → 0.01 from step 10000), not components reviving — as γ
shrinks, `φ(c) = c²/(c²+γ²)` scores ever-smaller CI values as active, so the same
decomposition measures higher. `NAlive_hidden` over the same span falls, which is the
cross-check.

### `floor` — the hidden net is raised to meet the output net

```yaml
pd:
  hidden_ci_floor: {sharpness: 10.0}
eval:
  metrics: [..., {type: HiddenCIShortfallLoss, violation_threshold: 0.1}]
```

`CI_hidden = smooth_max(raw_hidden_logit, CI_output_logit.detach())`, applied in **logit**
space inside `ComponentModel.calc_causal_importances*`. Logit space because both sigmoid
branches are monotone, so one ordering there is inherited by the mask *and* the minimality
penalty at once. Smooth rather than hard because a hard max zeroes the gradient wherever it
binds, stranding any hidden logit that fell under the floor.

**Why it exists:** the first mechanism built, and now the *contrast* rather than the
candidate. Where it binds it resolves a violation by declaring the subcomponent
hidden-important — the ordering holds, but the anomaly it was meant to surface gets papered
over. The output logits are detached, so the hidden objective's sparsity pressure cannot
reach the output net.

The shortfall metric rides along eval-only and reads **exactly 0.0** at every eval, which is
the self-check that the constraint is live rather than silently absent.

### `cap` — the output net is lowered to meet the hidden net

```yaml
pd:
  output_ci_cap: {sharpness: 10.0}
eval:
  metrics: [..., {type: HiddenCIShortfallLoss, violation_threshold: 0.1}]
```

`CI_output = smooth_min(raw_output_logit, CI_hidden_logit)` — the exact mirror, and the
same inequality. The hidden logits are **deliberately not detached**: that gradient path is
the mechanism.

**Why it exists — this is the primary arm.** Where the cap binds, the *output* objective's
gradient is redirected into the hidden logit (verified in tests: ~100% transfers when
binding, ~0% when slack). So to rely on a subcomponent, the output reconstruction must first
convince the hidden net that it does real work at its own matrix's output — and the hidden
importance-minimality penalty sets the price. A component that moves the logits through
anything other than its own site's activations cannot be bought. That is the hidden
objective *guiding* the output decomposition, rather than merely agreeing with it.

Being structural it also adds no force to the loss landscape, so unlike a penalty there is
nothing to out-pull impmin near zero (see the cancelled `sf` arms below).

Early confirmation that the two mechanisms are genuine mirror images: at step 0
`impmin_hidden` reads **2458** under the cap — the unconstrained control's value, i.e. the
hidden net untouched — against **3008** under the floor, which inflates it immediately.

### `sf1e-2-att` — the soft version, both nets moving

```yaml
pd:
  loss_metrics: [..., {type: HiddenCIShortfallLoss, coeff: 1e-2, violation_threshold: 0.1}]
```

`Σ_sites ( Σ_components relu(CI_out - CI_hidden) ) / n_positions`, on the `lower_leaky`
branch, with **neither side detached**, so gradients are equal and opposite: the correction
splits between raising the hidden CI and lowering the output CI.

Normalised exactly like the impmin losses (sum over components, mean over positions) so the
two coefficients are directly comparable. `1e-2` sits above impmin's per-entry gradient
throughout this schedule (impmin runs 6.5e-5 early → ~6.5e-3 at peak → 3.3e-3 at the end).

**Why it exists:** the same coefficient as the cancelled detached run, so the pair isolates
one variable — whether *attaching* `CI_out` fixes the always-active pathology that the
detached version showed.

## Cancelled runs

### `addsub-L18-12-allroute*` — 4 arms, jobs 8342–8345, cancelled at ~10 h

Base was `addsub-L18-11-press2-trunk` (the **1x** impmin recipe), plus a routing change:
`StochasticHiddenReconSubsetLoss.routing: {type: all}` instead of `uniform_k_subset`.

| run | job | the extra field |
|---|---|---|
| `addsub-L18-12-allroute` | 8342 | — (baseline for the routing change) |
| `addsub-L18-12-allroute-floor` | 8343 | `hidden_ci_floor` |
| `addsub-L18-12-allroute-sf1e-3` | 8344 | shortfall `coeff 1e-3` |
| `addsub-L18-12-allroute-sf1e-2` | 8345 | shortfall `coeff 1e-2` (never started) |

**Why cancelled.** The routing change was solving a non-problem. Subsetting is an
optimization aid, not a different objective — a site's expected error is the same either
way, subsetting just conditions the gradient. Adopting all-routing would only have moved
every hidden-recon number onto a new scale, forcing a fresh baseline arm for no gain. With
routing left alone, the finished `trunk-imp2x` run is already the matched control, which is
why the replacement series needs three arms rather than four.

The `{type: all}` routing option stays merged and available for a run that deliberately
wants maximal compounding; it is simply not used.

### `addsub-L18-13-imp2x-sf1e-3` / `-sf1e-2` — jobs 8466/8467, cancelled at ~6.5 h

The shortfall penalty with `CI_out` **detached**, at coefficients 1e-3 and 1e-2.

**Why cancelled — two reasons, one empirical and one conceptual.**

Empirically they grew extra always-active components, dose-dependently:

| at step 3000 | floor | sf1e-3 | sf1e-2 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| impmin_hidden (≈ active components) | 65.6 | 67.3 | **88.3** |
| recon | 0.00403 | 0.00404 | **0.00484** |

`sf1e-2` is worse on *both* axes — more active components **and** worse reconstruction —
which is a distorted objective rather than a sparsity/faithfulness trade-off. The mechanism:
SmoothL0's `φ(c) = c²/(c²+γ²)` is deliberately **flat at zero** (`φ'(0)=0`, so there is no
gradient cliff where most components live), while a linear hinge has a **constant** gradient
that does not fade as CI → 0. In precisely the regime that decides whether a component dies,
the penalty pushes up with full force and impmin pushes down with almost none. Choosing a
linear hinge was a mistake: it was reasoned about in isolation instead of against the
sparsity term it has to coexist with.

Conceptually, detaching `CI_out` forced every violation to be read as "the hidden net is
under-rating this" — resolvable only by inflating the hidden CI. The reading worth having is
the opposite: the output reconstruction should stop leaning on a subcomponent that does no
work at its site. That insight is what produced the `cap` arm, and the detach has since been
removed from the penalty entirely.

## Operational notes

The `cap` arm was interrupted three times. None was a code or model problem, but each cost
something and two are worth not repeating.

| job | ended by | cost |
|---|---|---|
| 8955 | requeued + held by another session at step 5085 | would have been 4.5 h had it been released — the fresh-launch script restarts from step 0 |
| 9049 | `DUE TO TIME LIMIT` at step 5460 | ~30 min |
| 9102 | cancelled on request, GPUs needed elsewhere | none |

**Every interrupted write leaves a truncated `model_<step>.pth`** — 14.1 GB, 14.6 GB and
448 MB against the 16.5 GB a complete one measures, all three unreadable
(`zipfile.is_zipfile` → False). This matters because `resolve_run_files` picks the
highest-numbered checkpoint, so a truncated one silently becomes "latest" and breaks every
subsequent load of that run. Check for it after any kill; the `training_<step>.pth` resume
state is written earlier and has survived intact each time.

**`~/pd_scratch/resume_ddp.sbatch` is not usable for this series as shipped.** It points at
`Code/param-decomp/8B_targeted`, a worktree without `output_ci_cap` — resuming through it
fails at config parse, since `PDConfig` rejects unknown fields. It also carries
`--time=00:30:00`, `--gpus=4` and `--mem=200G`. The corrected copy is
`~/pd_scratch/monotone_ci/resume_ddp_trunk_local.sbatch`: this worktree, 24 h, 2 GPUs, 120 G,
matching `run_ddp.sbatch`.

**Releasing a held job is not a resume.** These jobs' command is the fresh-launch script, so
a plain `scontrol release` restarts from step 0 and overwrites the run dir. Cancel and
resubmit through the resume path instead.

## Open

- The **spike anomaly**: every CI-derived metric jumps at one eval and recovers, seen in
  both `sf` arms at step 2500 and again in the probe at step 2 — always a `slow_every`
  boundary. Absent from `floor` and `cap`. No mechanism found; the nontarget
  `delta_override` was ruled out (target metrics are computed and collected before that
  block). A 3-step probe cannot discriminate, since `NAlive` saturates at 6144 there.
- Whether the **floor** should also drop its detach. Unlike the penalty, removing it there
  would route *every* hidden gradient — reconstruction and impmin alike — into the output
  net wherever the floor binds, which is a far broader coupling than the violation-driven
  correction. Deserves its own decision rather than being inherited.
- `HiddenCIShortfallLoss` also runs on the **nontarget** pass, where impmin is scaled 2x by
  `impmin_coeff_ratio` but the penalty is not — so the constraint fights a stronger sparsity
  pressure there at the same coefficient.
